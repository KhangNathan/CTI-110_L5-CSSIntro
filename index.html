Overview

After watching all my friends switch from consoles to PCs and all the people saying how keyboard & mouse is so much better for competitive gaming than controller, I was pulled into the rabbit hole on PCs and computers. At such a young age, everything in the computer looked so complicated and intricate that I thought it would be so difficult to build my own PC, so I began my journey by looking at only prebuilts. At this time, I had not a single clue what build a PC would be like at all. I didn't know what the parts were, how to connect it to other devices, or even what the acroynym PC stood for. So I asked all my friends what prebuilt company and model they went with. But seeing the prices to the performance, I was extremely confused why people wouldn't just pay for a console, which had similar performance and was much cheaper. For a while after that, I lost interest in PCs until I went to WECIB. That is where my passion for technology really grew and with my first class in CompTIA IT Fundamentals and actually learning about the different parts and what they did, my desire for my own PC flourished once again. But this time, I met someone who would be a huge help in my search for my own PC, Yash. Me and Yash weren't very close during the first semester of freshmen year, heck we weren't even really friends, but eventually we began to hang out some and he told me about his own journey with PCs, selling his own custom builds for a profit. So with some aid from him while I looked for parts online and help from others when actually building it, I was able to have my first very own PC.

It would be helpful to also note that about 6 months before building my own PC, I got one from my uncle as a handmedown, however it was not very powerful and had some issues such as only ethernet connectivity and windows software issues. I was extremely happy once I got my first actual good PC, but technically it wasn't my first ever PC, just my first ever custom built one that I made myself.




Components - Performance or Aesthetics?

A common question in the PC building world is the split between aesthetics amd performance when making your own PC build. Some people only want to focus on the performance of their PC, how fast and smooth it can run games or programs, while other people put more emphasis on the looks of their PC, making it both run decently well and looking nice & clean. However, for most builds and designs that look good to many people, the prices tend to be higher, which is why a balance must be made. Would you rather have a nice looking and pretty good build or sacrafice some of the aesthetics budget to get a bit better performance? That's why finding the right components for each individual build is so important, but for the most basic of PCs, there are 8 things needed, which are:

  • Central Processing Unit (CPU)
  • Graphics Processing Unit (GPU)
  • Random Access Memory (RAM)
  • Storage (SSD or HDD)
  • Motherboard
  • Power Supply Unit (PSU)
  • Cooling Components
  • Case

To explain what each and every one of these components did and how they interact with each other would take way too long, but to summarize it some components do the actually processing of data, with components that hold the data for either temporary or long term usage. The other ones either connect everything to each other, power all the parts, cool down parts that do get hot, or hold everything nice and neat. It's also worth mentioning the peripherals, such as keyboard, mouse, monitor, headphones, etc. These parts are required to interact with the PC and often times don't come with the components unless it is a prebuilt.
